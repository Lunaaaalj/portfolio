Traffic risk in metropolitan Monterrey

A data pipeline and a hierarchical Bayesian model that turn the 379,294 traffic incidents recorded by INEGI in the 18 municipalities of the Monterrey metropolitan area between 2019 and 2024 into a forecast for each one-kilometre cell. Risk is concentrated and persistent: 5% of the cells hold 43% of incidents, and the 2019 ranking of cells correlates 0.83 with that of 2024. The dominant source of error is how each municipality reports, not the statistics.

Reports, not crashes

Nothing here is a crash. Each grain stands for reports: forms filled in by each municipality’s traffic police and gathered by INEGI, 1,317,810 of them from 2019 to 2024.

The analysis keeps the 18 municipalities of the Monterrey metropolitan area: 379,294 reports, 28.8% of the country’s.

The same 18 report in all six years, so the years can be compared. Nationally, coverage runs from 91 municipalities in 2019 to 198 in 2024.

A report does not say where the vehicles met. It names a crossing: 96.6% are pinned to one, so the grains pile up on 54,742 points instead of spreading along the road.

What exists is a count at each crossing, not a scatter of points, which rules out flat-plane methods such as kernel density.

No report is deleted and nothing is imputed. Blank answers are not random: in fatal crashes the breath test goes unrecorded 2.4 times as often, so dropping them would bias against the worst cases.
