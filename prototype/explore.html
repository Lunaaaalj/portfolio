Design exploration

Options for the opening viewport, type, signature elements and motion. Each one stays inside the journal-and-survey-plate world from DESIGN.md; the grandeur comes from scale, structure and a few authored moments, not from added effects.

One decision this forces: DESIGN.md §8 says “nothing moves on load”. Options A3 and M1–M2 below add one authored moment per page. Choosing them means amending §8.

Opening viewport

The first screen of the home page. Today it is a name and three paragraphs; the work starts below the fold. Each option puts evidence in the first viewport.

A

Survey plate

The first screen is one drawing sheet: a zone-marked border, the GeoStats hex map as the drawn field, the name set large in the field, and a title block in the corner that indexes the three sheets. Strength: the most distinctive and the most "grand"; it is the site's signature used once at full size. Risk: it leans on one project's figure; the reforestation and Casa Monarca work enter only through the index.

B

Frontispiece

A scientific book's frontispiece: one figure printed large and bleeding off the right edge, with the name, role and abstract set against it and a caption hung beside the figure. Strength: the artifact leads, with less structure than A; it is quieter but still monumental. Risk: closer to a familiar "hero image" layout; it relies on type quality to avoid looking like a landing page.

C

Journal masthead and contents

The opening of a journal issue: a double-ruled masthead, the name as the title at very large size, then an abstract and a table of contents whose entries run on dotted leaders to their sheet numbers. Strength: all three projects are in the first viewport with equal weight; pure typography, so it is fast and robust. Risk: no figure above the fold; the grandeur depends entirely on the display type.

Type

The same passage set three ways. Grandeur in type comes from a display cut at large size with tight tracking, not from adding a decorative face.

T1

Source Serif 4 at display size (current)

Keeps DESIGN.md

The pinned face pushed harder: the optical-size axis at 60 for the name and headings, so the display cut has finer serifs and more contrast. Strength: no change to the system. Risk: least distinctive of the three.

T2

STIX Two, the scientific publishing face

My pick

STIX Two was drawn for scientific publishers (AIP, IEEE and others) and ships with a matching maths font, so the text and the equations come from one design, which fixes the thin Computer Modern problem from the review. Strength: a reason no other face has. Risk: KaTeX cannot use STIX Two Math; maths would move to MathJax rendered at build time, which is heavier to build but not to load.

T3

Bodoni Moda for display, Source Serif for text

A modern face with extreme contrast for the name and section heads only, the look of nineteenth-century engineering and survey title pages; body text stays Source Serif. Strength: the most ceremonial and "grand". Risk: high-contrast display serifs are now a common generated-portfolio look; it needs large sizes to hold up, and its hairlines thin out in dark mode.

Signature elements

Components that carry the world through every page. They are structural, so they also work without motion.

E1

Figure plate

Figures sit inside corner registration marks instead of a box, with a numbered caption. Lighter than a frame, and clearly a plate.

Fig. 2. Illustrative comparison on a staggered grid: ten species in four genera, assigned at random (left) and by a competition-aware rule (right). Red edges mark same-genus neighbours.

E2

Numbered section heads

On project pages only, where the numbers are real cross-references ("see §3, constraint 6"), as in a paper. The home page keeps plain heads.

E3

Equation with a linked margin note

Hover the equation

Pointing at a numbered equation draws a leader to its plain-language note and brings the note to full ink. This is the margin system made visible, the moment where both audiences meet.

Motion

Each study animates the real figure, and each motion says something about the data. Every one plays once, from an already-visible final state, and is instant under reduced motion.

M1

Field fills by value

Hex cells arrive from the lowest count to the highest; the red top-percentile cells land last, so the eye ends where the risk is.

M2

Constraint matrix plotted row by row

A plotter line sweeps down the constraint families in the order the formulation lists them: demand, calendar, recipes, lots, inventory, terminal.

M3

Same-genus contacts resolved

The red contact edges in the competition-aware grid fade out to its final count while the counter runs down from 182 to 57. This previews the reforestation page's interactive figure.

Same-genus contacts: 57

M4

Entry opens into its sheet

Navigating from the home page to a project, the thumbnail grows into Fig. 1, the title rises into the page title, and the small title block unfolds into the full one, using the browser's View Transitions with no library. This is the grandest transition and it explains where you went. Astro supports it natively.

Recommended combination

  • A, the survey plate, as the home opening, with motion A3 on first load only.
  • T2, STIX Two, for text and maths, with Plex Sans for notes and Plex Condensed in title blocks.
  • E1–E3 throughout the project pages.
  • M4 between the home page and the project pages; M1–M3 once per figure, on first view.
